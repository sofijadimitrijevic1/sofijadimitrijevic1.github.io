About me

I’m a master’s student in Computer Science at the University of California, Santa Barbara, focusing on machine learning. I’m interested in natural language processing, computational linguistics, and building useful, reliable AI systems.

From August 2025 to July 2026, I was a research assistant at Cal Poly on the Digital Democracy project. My work explored how to identify speakers in legislative testimony using self-introductions, combining interpretable language features with transformer models. I presented this research at CSCSU and the Applied AI Research Conference at Ai4.

I’ve also worked on production AI agents at Microsoft and machine learning systems at HP. Before UCSB, I earned a B.S. in Computer Science with an AI/ML concentration and a minor in Linguistics at Cal Poly, graduating magna cum laude.

Research papers

Detection of Self-Introductions in Legislative Testimony

Sofija Dimitrijevic, Pallavi Das, Kasey Liu, Foaad Khosmood

AAIRC · Ai4 2026Expanded study · arXiv preprint

A lightweight pipeline for identifying speakers in legislative hearings. Combining discourse features and fine-tuned BERT probabilities with XGBoost improved F1 from 0.9747 to 0.9782 and reduced test errors from 241 to 207.

Detection of Self-Introductions in Legislative Testimony

Sofija Dimitrijevic, Foaad Khosmood

CSCSU 2026Original study · unarchived manuscript

Built a training dataset from a corpus of 1.54 million utterances across five state legislative sessions. Compared decision trees, random forests, and XGBoost using lexical, positional, structural, and introductory-phrase features; XGBoost achieved 0.9704 F1.
